Topology: Linear LDO regulator with passive protection. A USB-C receptacle feeds a reverse-polarity FET plus a load-switch/inrush controller, then a 3.3V LDO supplies the LEDs and the power indicator. LEDs are directly driven from the 3.3V rail through individual current-limiting resistors. No microcontroller, no switching, no PD — just the simplest reliable path from 5V USB-C to 3.3V LEDs.
  A 555 or microcontroller would add unnecessary complexity, cost, and quiescent current for a fixed-function LED driver. A switching regulator would violate the LDO requirement and add EMI/noise. A discrete multivibrator is irrelevant here. The LDO approach is the cheapest, most available, and most predictable solution: one regulator, a few passives, and standard jellybean parts. Reverse-polarity is handled by a P-channel MOSFET in series with VBUS (no diode drop), and inrush is limited by a small series resistor plus the LDO’s enable pin capacitor for soft-start.
  Rejected: 555 timer — no timing function needed, adds cost and complexity; Microcontroller (e.g. ATtiny) — overkill for fixed LED drive, adds firmware, cost, and quiescent current; Switching regulator (buck) — violates LDO requirement, adds EMI and component count; Schottky diode for reverse polarity — introduces ~0.3V drop and power dissipation, worse than a FET; Direct 5V LED drive — violates 3.3V regulation requirement

Rails:
  VBUS_5V  5V — USB-C input after reverse-polarity and inrush protection, feeds LDO input
  VREG_3V3  3.3V — LDO output, powers all three indicator LEDs, power LED, and test point

Calculations:
  LED current-limiting resistor: R = (V_supply - V_led) / I_led = (3.3V - 2.0V) / 0.005A = 260Ω → use 200Ω (nearest standard 5% value, gives ~6.5mA, within safe range for 0805 LED and 0603 resistor)
  Inrush resistor power: P = I²R = (0.5A)² × 10Ω = 2.5W → use 2W resistor (conservative, short-duration pulse, 2W rating sufficient for brief inrush)
  LDO output current: 3 LEDs × 6.5mA + 1 power LED × 6.5mA = ~26mA total load, well within MIC5205’s 150mA rating
  LDO power dissipation: Pd = (Vin - Vout) × Iout = (5V - 3.3V) × 0.026A = 0.046W → negligible, no heatsink needed
  Reverse-polarity FET: BSS84A with 10kΩ gate pull-down ensures off state when VBUS absent; when VBUS present, Q1 conducts with Rds_on < 5Ω, drop < 250mV at 500mA

Layout constraints:
  USB-C connector J1 must be at one short edge (e.g., bottom edge) of the 30×20mm board
  4-pin header J2 must be at the opposite short edge (top edge)
  2-pin test point header J3 must be accessible on top layer, near VREG_3V3 rail
  All four indicator LEDs (D1–D4) and their resistors (R3–R6) must be in a linear row along one long edge (e.g., right edge) for visibility when board is installed
  All components on top layer only — no bottom-side parts
  Silkscreen designators on every part — ensure adequate clearance and readability
  Decoupling capacitors C2 and C4 must be placed within 2mm of LDO pins U1
  Bulk capacitors C1 and C3 must be placed adjacent to U1 input/output pins
  Q1 (reverse-polarity FET) must be placed between J1 (VBUS) and U1 (LDO input), with minimal trace length
  R2 (inrush resistor) must be in series between Q1 drain and U1 input
  Board outline: 30mm × 20mm, 2-layer FR4, 0.8mm thickness preferred for rigidity

Risks:
  USB-C connector footprint may not be in standard library — verify J1 footprint matches available USB-C receptacle (e.g., Molex 217128-0001 or equivalent) before layout
  BSS84A in SOT-23 is hand-solderable but small — ensure adequate silkscreen and pad design for prototyping
  200Ω resistors give 6.5mA instead of 5mA — acceptable but verify LED brightness is not too dim; if needed, 220Ω gives ~5.5mA
  Inrush resistor R2 at 10Ω and 2W is large for 0603 — consider 1206 or 1210 footprint if thermal concerns arise; 2W in 0603 is possible but marginal
  MIC5205-3.3BM5 in SOT-223 requires careful thermal pad layout — ensure copper pour on tab for heat dissipation even though power is low
  Tolerance stack-up: 3.3V LDO ±2% + LED Vf variation ±0.2V + resistor 1% → LED current could vary ±15% — acceptable for indicator use
  USB-C receptacle pinout must be verified — only VBUS (A4/B9) and GND (A1/B12) are used; ensure no data pins are connected or left floating in a way that causes issues
  If downstream board pulls LED lines low, ensure LDO can sink current — MIC5205 can sink/supply, so OK
  Board size 30×20mm is tight with USB-C connector (~8mm depth) + 4-pin header (~10mm) + 4 LEDs + passives — verify component placement fits with 1mm minimum trace/space
